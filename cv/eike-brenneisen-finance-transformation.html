Dear Finance Transformation team,

Ten years ago I left Australia with an expiring Work and Holiday visa and a fairly specific promise to myself: come back one day, but next time with something worth bringing. This application is me keeping it.

Financial services is going through more change at once than at almost any point I can remember. Regulation keeps tightening, demographics are reshaping both the workforce and the customer base, and above all technology and AI are rewriting how finance functions are actually run. They are not making the old model faster; they are replacing it. It is a rare wave, and I am swimming at the front of it. In practice that means I no longer hand a finance leader a deck about their future steering model: I hand them a working tool, built with AI on their own data in days, where they move the parameters themselves and watch the P&L react. The conversation is a different one when the numbers are live in the room. I am a Manager in Banking & Capital Markets Transformation at Deloitte Germany, and I would like to ride that wave with your practice.

Underneath it sit three things. The first is practical grounding in the finance function itself. I trained as a certified bank clerk, advised private and corporate clients on financing and investments, and worked in accounting through year-end close before moving into consulting via a CEMS Master in International Management. I know how finance and accounting processes behave in daily operation, not only in a target picture. That is why my recommendations survive contact with the people who run them.

The second is consulting delivery. I have led integrated finance and performance transformation programs end to end, from operating model design, process improvement and data foundations to technology enablement, through multiple go-lives and migrations, reporting directly to the board. I have worked on several large post-merger integrations, most recently as sub-project lead for compensation harmonisation in a major insurance merger, where the task was to make an unpopular change land by showing precisely what it meant for each person affected. Planning, forecasting and scenario simulation, profitability and cost management, and management reporting are the substance of that work. Alongside it I lead teams, and the part I enjoy most is coaching people into ownership earlier than they expect.

The third is tooling, and increasingly AI. I have made a habit of moving into whatever is next, and right now that is AI hands-on: my current engagement is the implementation of AI-enabled sales processes at a major insurer: real operations, not a pilot deck. Before that I established a new AI use-case approach in my practice, with functional prototypes built on the client's own data instead of slide decks, and grew a portfolio of tools around it. From late September I am presenting a firm-wide webinar series on the AI use-case model behind it. What makes this work is that I sit on both sides at once, grounded in the business and fluent in how software actually gets built: close enough to the business to know what is genuinely needed, and close enough to delivery to build it.

What I am looking for now is to take that practical, consulting and tooling experience international: to learn how finance transformation is done in the Australian market, and to contribute what I have built in Germany to your practice. That exchange, in both directions, is the real reason for this application. I would welcome the chance to discuss where I could be most useful.

Kind regards,
Eike Brenneisen
